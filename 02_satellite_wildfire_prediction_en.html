Social Impact 02 · Disaster Remote Sensing

Multi-source Satellite Fusion for Wildfire Detection and Spread Prediction

Study a multi-source spatio-temporal remote-sensing framework that jointly predicts active fire, burned area, and next-day wildfire spread for early warning, resource planning, and risk explanation.

Input: satellite + weather + terrain + fuelMiddle: spatio-temporal fusion / physics priorOutput: fire, burned area, spread probability

The value of wildfire prediction is not only detecting what is already burning, but anticipating where risk may spread and explaining which environmental factors drive the prediction.

Student Research View

Disaster AI with public-safety value

This topic is suitable for students interested in remote sensing, climate, disaster response, and multimodal AI. It has rich public datasets, clear experiments, and a natural social-impact story.

Research question

How can a model predict both where wildfire is and where it may spread?

Model active fire, burned area, and next-day spread as related tasks rather than isolated static classifications.

Contribution

Multi-source spatio-temporal fusion

Fuse reflectance, thermal anomalies, weather, terrain, vegetation, and fuel variables, with wind and slope as physical priors.

Expected output

Risk prediction and explanation loop

Multi-task baselines, next-day spread probability maps, lead-time recall, AUPRC, risk-factor visualization, and a conference-style paper.

Input / Output

Inputs, intermediate results, and final artifacts

The project emphasizes multi-source fusion and multi-task outputs.

01
Input data

TS-SatFire, Next Day Wildfire Spread, FIRMS, MODIS / VIIRS, and WildfireSpreadTS.

02
Temporal fusion

ConvLSTM, Temporal U-Net, UTAE, or spatio-temporal Transformer over historical observations.

03

Wind speed, wind direction, slope, vegetation moisture, fuel type, and drought index.

04

Active-fire probability, burned-area mask, next-day spread probability, calibration, and risk-factor maps.

Research Position

Core problem

Emergency response needs to know not only where fire already exists, but where it may spread, whether risk probabilities are calibrated, and which factors explain the prediction.

This topic unifies fire detection, burned-area mapping, and spread prediction under a multi-task learning framework.

Research Path

Research path

Each stage has a concrete intermediate artifact.

Stage ATask and data

Understand fire labels, burned-area labels, spread labels, environmental variables, and class imbalance.

Stage BSingle-task baselines

Reproduce Random Forest, U-Net, DeepLabV3+, or ConvLSTM baselines.

Stage CMulti-source fusion

Add weather, terrain, vegetation, fuel, and multi-day inputs.

Stage DMulti-task and physics priors

Jointly predict fire, burned area, and spread while injecting wind and slope priors.

Stage EGeneralization and explanation

Test across years, regions, ecological zones, and extreme-weather scenarios.

Research Details

Core technical components

The method centers on multi-source encoding, temporal prediction, class imbalance, and interpretation.

Multi-source Encoder

Encode optical imagery, thermal anomalies, vegetation indices, weather, terrain, and fuel variables.

Spatio-temporal Prediction

Use historical fire and environmental changes to predict next-day newly burning areas.

Physics-guided Interpretation

Use wind, slope, and fuel conditions with SHAP, Grad-CAM, or attention visualization.

Evaluation

Evaluation

Wildfire pixels are sparse, so evaluation should focus on recall, precision, calibration, and early prediction.

Metric / ObservationTechnical meaningIntuitive explanation
Precision / RecallFalse alarms and missed fire regions.Does the model find real fire while avoiding excessive false alarms?
F1 / IoUOverlap between predicted and true fire or burned areas.Are predicted areas spatially reasonable?
AUPRCRanking quality under severe class imbalance.Are truly dangerous areas ranked near the top?
Lead-time RecallRecall for areas predicted one or more days in advance.Can the model identify risk before the fire arrives?
Calibration ErrorAgreement between predicted probability and observed frequency.Does an 80% risk score behave like an 80% risk?
Cross-region PerformanceGeneralization across years, regions, and ecological zones.Can the method transfer beyond the training region?
